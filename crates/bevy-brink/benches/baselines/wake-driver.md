# Scenario harness — WAKE-driver baselines (BH-4, issue #973)

Generated by `cargo bench -p bevy-brink --bench scenario_bench -- --mode wake`. The reactive-sleep wake contract (`docs/effects-spec.md` §13.1): a `FlowSleep` policy parks a flow at its natural `-> DONE` yield; a **parked** flow is skipped by Collect (it steps zero times, §13.1 point 1), and the plugin's `mark_wake_dirty` → `run_flow_sleep` systems re-evaluate its pure condition — only when a dependency moved (`#913` detect verdict) — and wake it only on condition-true. Column mapping: `frame p50/p99` is the whole reactive frame (the wake systems over the sleeping population plus, on a `drive_batch` row, the batch turn that steps only the active/woken flows); `step p50` is the timed `advance_batch` turn alone (0 on an idle-wake row that runs no batch turn); `collect`/`apply` read 0. **Two regimes — read them differently.** (1) The `wake-idle-*` rows run **no** batch turn: they isolate the pure per-frame cost of the wake systems over a purely-sleeping population on a quiet World, and are the clean **zero-cost-parked** measurement. `wake-idle-detect-*` are detect-capable (re-evaluated only on a World change — none here, so after one bootstrap evaluation they cost only a query scan); `wake-idle-poll-*` force the must-poll cadence (`#996` interim: a component-backed condition re-evaluates every frame). These are linear in the sleeping count and are the headline. (2) The `wake-*-{90to10,99to1}` and `wake-storm-*` rows **do** run the batch turn: a woken/active flow steps to `-> DONE`, which fires the plugin's `gc_on_turn_done` handle-registry observer — and that observer sweeps **every flow under M** (T1d reachable-handle GC), i.e. it is **O(total flows) per `-> DONE`**. So a `drive_batch` row's cost is O(active × total) and a wake-storm is O(n²); at these sizes the gc sweep, **not** the wake systems, dominates the frame. This is a T1d handle-GC interaction orthogonal to the wake contract (the batch-serial baselines avoid it only because their synthetic story parks at a *choice*, never `-> DONE`); it is called out in the hand-maintained header and is the reason `wake-storm` is capped at 1k. See `docs/bevy-bench.md` for the shared honesty caveats.

### Headline — zero-cost-parked (the §13.1 point-1 contract)

The `wake-idle-*` rows drive a purely-**sleeping** population with **no** batch
turn on a quiet World — the pure per-frame cost of the wake systems
(`mark_wake_dirty` + `run_flow_sleep`) with nobody waking. It is **linear** in
the sleeping count:

| sleeping flows | idle-detect frame p50 (ms) | idle-poll frame p50 (ms) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0.054 | — |
| 100 | 0.222 | 0.220 |
| 1,000 | 1.637 | 1.653 |
| 10,000 | 16.431 | 16.533 |

**Per-parked-flow overhead ≈ 1.6 µs/frame** — `(16.431 − 0.054) / 10 000 =
1.64 µs`, and `(1.637 − 0.054) / 1 000 = 1.58 µs` (the same slope, so it is
genuinely linear, not amortized). This is **not literally zero**, but it is a
flat per-sleeper bookkeeping scan — `run_flow_sleep` rebuilds its query and
walks every sleeper each frame — and, decisively, **a parked flow takes zero
steps**: it never appears in the `turns`/`step` columns, produces no lines,
and triggers no turn-boundary GC (see below). The Collect-skip contract holds
exactly (`turns_completed` counts only active/woken flows on every row). The
1.6 µs residual is a linear scan that a future optimization can shrink by
caching `run_flow_sleep`'s `QueryState` (it currently calls `world.query()`
afresh each frame).

**Must-poll vs detect-skip:** for this trivial condition (`~ return 0`) the two
idle sweeps are indistinguishable (`idle-poll-10k` 16.533 ms vs
`idle-detect-10k` 16.431 ms — a +0.01 µs/flow difference, inside the noise): the
per-sleeper **query scan dominates**, and re-evaluating a trivial pure function
every frame (the `#996` must-poll interim) adds almost nothing on top. The
must-poll penalty scales with the *condition's own* cost, not with the number
of sleepers — a heavier `is_player_nearby`-style condition would separate these
two curves. Detect-skip's value is therefore latent at these sizes and will
grow with condition weight, not flow count.

### The `drive_batch` rows are dominated by handle-GC, not by the wake systems

Every `wake-*-{90to10,99to1}` and `wake-storm-*` row runs the batch turn, so a
woken/active flow steps to `-> DONE` — and `-> DONE` fires the plugin's
`gc_on_turn_done` observer, whose reachable-handle sweep iterates **every flow
under `M`** (T1d GC). That makes each turn-boundary **O(total flows)**, so a
`drive_batch` frame is **O(active × total)** and a wake-storm is **O(n²)**:

| row | active | total | frame p50 (ms) | step p50 (ms) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| wake-1k-99to1 | 10 | 1,000 | 4.31 | 2.64 |
| wake-1k-90to10 | 100 | 1,000 | 28.58 | 27.00 |
| wake-10k-99to1 | 100 | 10,000 | 286.31 | 269.36 |
| wake-10k-90to10 | 1,000 | 10,000 | 2,690.43 | 2,674.99 |
| wake-storm-100 | 100 (woken) | 100 | 2.94 | 2.72 |
| wake-storm-1k | 1,000 (woken) | 1,000 | 271.01 | 269.28 |

Read the `step p50` column (the `advance_batch` turn alone): stepping the *same*
100 flows costs 30.7 µs at 100 total but 269 ms at 10 000 total — ~2.7 µs ×
total per step, i.e. the per-step cost tracks the **total** flow population, not
the work of the step. The `wake-storm` curve is unmistakably quadratic
(100 → 2.94 ms, 1 000 → 271 ms; ×92 for ×10), so a 10 000-flow storm
extrapolates to **~27 s/frame** (an 8-frame probe measured 26.7 s directly) —
which is why `wake-storm` is **capped at 1k** here. This is a **T1d handle-GC
interaction, orthogonal to the reactive-wake contract** — the wake systems
themselves are linear and cheap (the idle rows prove it). The batch-serial /
parallel baselines never hit it only because their synthetic story parks at a
*choice* (`WaitingForChoice`), never `-> DONE`. **This O(total)-per-`-> DONE`
GC sweep is the real scalability ceiling for large reactive-sleep populations
and is flagged as a follow-up** (a woken flow that touches no handles should not
pay a full-population reachable-handle sweep every turn).

### Comparison vs the batch / parallel baselines

The right cross-baseline reading is the **zero-cost-parked** one: `batch-10k`
steps 10 000 flows at **22.4 ms/frame** (`batch-serial-driver.md`), whereas
`wake-idle-detect-10k` holds 10 000 flows **parked** at **16.4 ms/frame** while
doing **zero** steps, zero output, and zero GC. So the wake driver's headline
win is structural — a mostly-parked population never enters Step, Apply, or the
turn-boundary GC — but the residual per-sleeper scan (~1.6 µs) is *comparable*
to a batch active step (~2.1 µs/flow), so "parked" buys you skipping the *turn*,
not a free frame. Where the same populations mix under a batch turn
(`wake-*-{90to10,99to1}`), the T1d GC term above dominates and those rows should
**not** be compared against the batch/parallel `frame p50` as if they measured
the wake path — they measure handle-GC.
